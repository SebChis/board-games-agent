## Overview
Number of players: Not explicitly stated, but the components support up to 5 players based on the 5 colors of meeples provided. Duration: Not explicitly specified. Victory objective: Gain the most points by the end of the game by strategically expanding the landscape and placing meeples.

## Components
- 72 Landscape tiles showing roads, cities, and monasteries in a field.
- 1 Start tile with a darker colored back.
- 1 Scoreboard.
- 40 regular meeples, including 8 in each of these colors: yellow, red, green, blue, and black.
- 5 abbots, 1 in each color, which are not important from the base game point of view.

## Game Setup
1. Place the start tile in the middle of the table.
2. Shuffle the remaining tiles and set them as facedown stacks accessible to all players, or place them into a sack or box.
3. Set the scoreboard to the side of your playing surface.
4. Distribute 7 meeples of the chosen color to each player to form their personal supply.
5. Place each player's eighth meeple on the 0 space on the scoreboard to track scores throughout the game.
6. The youngest player starts the game.

## Turn Overview
The game is played in clockwise order. The current player completes the following actions in order:

### 1. Placing a tile
- You must draw exactly one land tile from a stack and place it faceup adjacent to any tile already on the board.
- The tile must continue the existing landscape and illustration, such as a city segment connecting to an open city.
- If a tile cannot legally be placed anywhere, and all players agree, it is removed from the game and the player draws another.

### 2. Placing a meeple
- You may place a meeple from your personal supply onto the tile you just placed.
- You may not place a meeple in a feature where there already is at least one other meeple, including one of yours.

### 3. Scoring a feature
- You must score any feature completed by the tile placement.
- After each scoring, return the scored meeples to their owners' supply.
- If there are multiple meeples in a single scored feature, the player with the most meeples is awarded full points and all other players receive nothing.
- When more than one player has the most meeples in a scored feature, the tied players all score full points.

## Specific Rules and Exceptions
- **Roads:** You can place a meeple as a highwayman on a road. A road is completed when both ends lead to a village, a city, a monastery, or the road forms a loop. Each tile in a completed road is worth 1 point.
- **Cities:** You can place a meeple as a knight in a city. A city is completed when it is surrounded by walls and there are no gaps inside. Each tile in a completed city is worth 2 points, and each coat of arms in the completed city is worth an extra 2 points.
- **Monasteries:** You can place a meeple as a monk on a monastery. Monasteries are always depicted in the center of a tile. A monastery is completed when it is surrounded by 8 tiles. During scoring, the monastery is worth 1 point per tile that completes it, including the monastery itself, totaling 9 points.
- **Many meeples on the same feature:** You can legally place a meeple on an unconnected feature segment and later draw a tile that connects it to a feature already occupied by an opponent.
- **Immediate return of a meeple:** You can place a tile that completes a feature, deploy a meeple to that feature, score the feature immediately, and return the meeple to your supply. A player may only deploy one meeple per turn, and that meeple may be deployed only once before any scoring.

## Endgame Conditions and Scoring
- The game ends as soon as a player can no longer draw and place a landscape tile.
- At the end of the game, all meeples still in play are scored.
- Each incomplete road is worth 1 point per tile.
- Each incomplete city is worth 1 point per tile and 1 point per coat of arms.
- Each incomplete monastery is worth 1 point plus 1 point per adjacent tile.
- Once the final score is known, the winner is the player with the most points.
